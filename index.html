Commercial evaluation · like-for-like basis

Six vendors, one comparable basket.

Six vendors returned eight priced options between them — Infinity and Sattva each gave two — covering different areas, different inclusions, different tax positions and, in two cases, extra rooms nobody else was asked to price. This evaluation strips all of that back to a single common basket of wardrobe, loft, dressing unit and study table, then ranks on cost per square foot so the numbers actually mean the same thing.

01 · Method

Why the raw totals mislead

Ranking the options as received would be an error. Four adjustments are needed before any two numbers can be compared.

The adjustments

The quotes arrived covering areas from 122 to 153 sq.ft, with three vendors omitting a dressing unit entirely and two pricing rooms that were never part of the enquiry. Comparing the bottom lines as they stand would rank the vendor who quoted the least work, not the vendor who offers the best value.

Effect of normalising

Movement between the quoted subtotal and the adjusted like-for-like basket. Bars to the right mean the vendor looked cheaper than they are because scope was missing from their quote.

08 · Recommendation

Two routes worth taking forward

Neither route should be awarded on the current paperwork. Both need a revised written quote first.
Before awarding — ask every shortlisted vendor for this in writing
Basis of this evaluation. Every figure is taken from the vendor’s own document and reconciles to the total that vendor stated — three formal PDFs and four handwritten sheets. Where a vendor bundled lines, the bundle is preserved rather than split. Material specifications recorded as verbal are marked as such throughout and carry no contractual weight until confirmed in writing.
